Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping Pipeline — Spinal Cord Imaging

Complete — co-op research deliverable
Polytechnique Montréal / NeuroPoly

A 2D MRI post-processing pipeline that separates ex vivo spinal cord tissue susceptibility into iron (paramagnetic) and myelin (diamagnetic) components and benchmarks the result against histology, extending a technique well established in the brain into the comparatively unexplored spinal cord.

  • Built the full QSM chain: phase unwrapping, background field removal, dipole inversion, but adapted brain-optimized MATLAB toolboxes (SEPIA, STI-Suite) to single-slice ex vivo data
  • Implemented and tuned a 2D V-SHARP background-field removal method, improving CNR from 0.328 to 1.083
  • Compared three dipole-inversion methods (TKD, closed-form L2, MEDI) and optimized the TKD threshold for contrast
  • Applied two susceptibility source-separation algorithms (χ-separation, ANISO-QSM) plus myelin water fraction mapping with Otsu-thresholded T2 cutoff
  • Built a custom co-registration pipeline (Spinal Cord Toolbox + ANTs) to align histology and MWF maps to the MRI reference space
  • Validated statistically against histology: MWF vs. myelin volume fraction correlated at r = 0.97 (ROI-wise); ANISO-QSM outperformed χ-separation (r = 0.64–0.66 vs. 0.39–0.40) against myelin ground truth
ToolchainMATLAB (SEPIA, STI-Suite), Python, SCT, ANTs
AcquisitionsmeGRE (8 echo), MESE (32 echo)
Resultr = 0.64–0.66, p < 0.05 vs. histology

Automated Analytical Balance

Complete — personal / course project
Sensors & Instrumentation · Solo Project

A closed-loop optical balance for the 50–500g range: an IR emitter/dual-photodiode sensor detects beam displacement, and a stepper-driven counterweight is servoed to the balance point by a tuned PID loop running on an ESP32.

  • Designed the optical sensing stage: IR emitter, dual photodiodes, transimpedance + instrumentation amplifier signal conditioning
  • Iterated sensor gain empirically (TIA feedback resistor, INA gain) to trade off sensitivity vs. usable travel range
  • Drove a NEMA 11 stepper via a TMC2209 driver at 1/128 microstepping (1.28M positions over 100mm travel), using UART stall detection for self-calibrating zero-reference homing
  • Implemented a PID control loop converting sensor voltage error into stepper frequency, with a low-pass filtered derivative term to damp oscillation
  • Calibrated with a piecewise-linear steps-to-grams mapping from scientific reference weights
  • Achieved ±5g accuracy across the operating range, ±3g repeatability, and negligible drift over a 10-minute stability test

Analog Optical Heart Rate Monitor

Complete — team project
Electronic Circuits II · Team of 4

A fully analog, microcontroller-free photoplethysmography heart rate monitor: an IR LED/phototransistor sensor feeds a multi-stage op-amp chain that filters, amplifies, and thresholds the pulse signal to drive real-time high/low BPM alarms. I developed the enclosure and contributed to full-team circuit validation.

  • Built a 6-stage analog conditioning chain (MCP6002 op-amps): DC removal + bias, low-pass filtering, amplification, AC re-coupling, comparator, and an NE555 monostable pulse shaper
  • Designed an RC frequency-to-voltage converter and dual-comparator threshold detection to drive high/low-BPM alarm LEDs
  • Diagnosed and fixed a buzzer current-draw fault by switching to LED indicators after the regulator couldn't supply 50mA under battery power
  • Designed and 3D-printed a battery-powered enclosure with a spring-loaded finger clamp and labeled indicator LEDs
  • Validated to within 3 BPM of an Apple Smart Watch across rest, activity, and recovery, with ≤6 BPM drift over a 6-hour stability test
SensorLTE-302 IR LED / LTR-301 phototransistor
Signal chainMCP6002 op-amps, NE555 timer
AccuracyWithin 3 BPM of reference

Dual-Microprocessor Battleship

Complete — team project
Microprocessors & Digital Logic · Team of 4

A two-player, best-of-3 physical Battleship game built on two independent Arduino Uno controllers communicating over UART, each with its own LED grid, joystick targeting, and a discrete-logic 7-segment score display.

  • Designed a dual-Arduino state machine (ship selection → shooting/idle → hit/miss → reset) synchronized over UART with a defined integer command set
  • Built a discrete combinational logic circuit (74HC08/74HC32/74HC04) to decode a 2-bit signal into 4 score patterns, minimized via Boolean algebra to reduce gate count
  • Implemented joystick cursor control with dead-zone/threshold logic and animated hit/miss LED feedback with buzzer tones
  • Diagnosed a nondeterministic behavior bug traced to 9V battery sag under load, confirmed via multimeter and swap testing
MCUs2× Arduino Uno (ATmega328P)
LinkUART, 9600 baud
Logic74HC08 / 74HC32 / 74HC04
